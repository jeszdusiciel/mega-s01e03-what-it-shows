Two problems, two fixes

Parser: the shared parser reads month first even for day-first tenants. For example, R-41200 describes a 7 April reminder arriving for 4 July. Parse using the tenant’s explicit format, reject invalid dates, and preserve date-only values without a timezone conversion. Cover both app and email paths and both booking-form versions in regression checks. The parser ticket includes the five uncertain reports listed below as filed associations, not five proven instances of month/day reversal.

Hint: booking-v3 shows MM/DD/YYYY to day-first tenants while its form reads the tenant’s day-first format. Users following the hint get the wrong date even if the shared parser is fixed. Generate the displayed hint and the form’s interpretation from one tenant format. Check v3 form submissions separately from v2 forms and app/email.

The public repository’s tickets/parser.md and tickets/hint.md hold the ticket bodies; each lists all assigned report IDs so its count can be audited.

Read the uncertainty, too

The five reports in desk-notes.json do not establish another shared cause. R-41202 and R-41620 describe a one-day shift, R-41344 differs between Lisbon and Toronto, R-41209 affects June, and R-41236 claims an invite preceded its booking. They are filed under parser by the supplied tenant/channel rule. That rule does not prove their cause. Keep the originals attached and investigate timezone handling, stored dates, event IDs, and send timestamps. Do not invent a third fix from a common subject or a vague symptom.

The queue is still working

Received time and merge time describe different events. The July merge spike does not date the start of the failure, and “known” does not mean fixed. Keep both tickets open, route new reports by the failing path, and monitor received counts separately from merge counts. Recheck a fresh export before treating this snapshot as current.

Change what the desk does next

The accepted decision 0001 matched subjects alone. Its consequences mention splitting one bug across subjects but omit the reverse: unrelated bugs can share a subject and lose their own ticket. Its review date is old; the live explorer already uses cause, not subject.

Use decision 0002, which replaces that rule without rewriting the accepted historical record. Read the report, resolve the tenant, locale, form and channel, and compare the failing path. Same subject is neither necessary nor sufficient. Missing context or contradictory symptoms require investigation, not an automatic merge. Reconcile counts after each new export, keeping one ID per report.

This review covers the supplied export through 4 October 2026. It does not claim that production is repaired or that later reports are already classified.
